Privacy Policy

Rootfast — Grove Defense  ·  Last updated: September 19, 2026

Overview

Rootfast — Grove Defense is a mobile base-defense strategy game developed by Tinker Apps. Tinker Apps does not operate its own game servers and does not intentionally collect or store gameplay-related personal data on its own infrastructure. However, Tinker Apps chooses to integrate certain third-party services (such as Google services) that process data as described in this policy. Tinker Apps determines which of these services are integrated and, to that extent, may act as a data controller for data collected through them; Google also processes data under its own policies. Tinker Apps does not sell personal data for money. All Google services referenced in this policy operate under Google's Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy).

Data We Collect

Tinker Apps does not operate its own game servers and does not intentionally collect or store gameplay-related personal data on its own infrastructure. Your game progress — including campaign progress and stars, Heartseeds and Grove upgrades, faction unlocks, any run in progress, daily-challenge and endless-mode records, and settings — is stored locally on your device using standard application storage.

If you sign in to Google Play Games, your long-term progress is also saved to the cloud using Google Play Saved Games so it can be restored after a reinstall or on a new device (see Google Play Games Services below). ⚠️ The cloud save holds your meta progression only — Heartseeds, Grove upgrades, campaign results and stars, records and unlocked factions. A run in progress and your app settings are device-local and are not synced: an unfinished run does not follow you to another device. If you do not sign in, your progress stays only on your device, and clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app will erase it (the non-consumable full-game purchase can still be restored through Google Play — see below). Game data is designed not to include directly identifying personal information.

If you contact us directly — for example, by email for support or to make a privacy request — we will receive the information you choose to provide, such as your email address and the contents of your message, and we use it only to respond to and handle your request.

Advertising

Rootfast contains no advertising. There is no advertising SDK in the app, no banner, interstitial or rewarded ad, and no Android Advertising ID is read, collected or shared. Nothing in the app is served or measured by an ad network.

Earlier versions of Rootfast did display ads through Google AdMob. That was removed in September 2026 when the app moved to a free preview with a single optional purchase, and with it went the ad-related consent form that used to appear in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. If you are updating from an older version, no advertising identifier is collected by the current app.

Your Analytics & Diagnostics Choice

Because the app carries no advertising, there is exactly one privacy choice to make, and the app asks you directly rather than through an advertising consent form. Every player is shown a card about it on one of their first launches, telling them what is collected and how to stop it. Your answer controls the Firebase Analytics section below in full. It controls one part of the Firebase Crashlytics section — whether your Play Games Player ID is attached to crash reports — but it does not switch crash reporting itself on or off.

What that card does depends on where you are, because the law does:

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, it is a request, and nothing is collected until you agree. Analytics storage stays switched off from the moment the app starts — before the card appears, while it is on screen, and permanently if you decline.
  • Everywhere else, anonymous gameplay statistics are collected from first launch, and the card tells you so and offers a single tap to stop it. You are never opted in silently: the card appears whether or not you go looking for it, and the Settings switch below turns it off at any time, in any country.

In both cases the same rules apply once you have answered:

  • If you decline, analytics storage is switched off: Firebase Analytics does not read or store analytics identifiers on your device, and no Play Games Player ID is attached to analytics or crash reports. A refusal is remembered and you are not asked again.
  • If you agree, gameplay events are collected as described below, and — if you are signed in to Google Play Games — your Player ID is attached to them and to crash reports.
  • Crash reports are collected either way. Crash and error diagnostics are sent whether or not you agree, because a crash report is how a broken build gets fixed. Declining keeps them anonymous rather than switching them off; see Firebase Crashlytics below for exactly what one contains.
  • The three advertising-related consent categories that Google's consent framework defines (ad storage, ad user data, ad personalisation) are set to denied at all times and are never granted by this choice, because the app does no advertising.

You can change your answer at any time, in any country, under Settings > Share anonymous diagnostics. Turning it off takes effect immediately and clears your Play Games Player ID from both Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics; see Data Deletion for what that does and does not remove. Where Google Play indicates that a player is a minor, the app does not ask this question and does not enable analytics identification at all (see Age Signals below).

Google Play Billing & In-App Purchases

In-app purchases are processed entirely by Google Play. Tinker Apps does not receive or store any payment information. All purchase validation is handled by Google under Google's Privacy Policy.

Rootfast is a free preview — Sprout Isle and the first three Grove levels, replayable without limit — with a single optional purchase:

  • Rootfast — Full Game — a one-time, non-consumable purchase that unlocks the remaining campaign levels and the Endless, Daily Challenge and Ascension modes. The two additional factions, Thornfang and Emberkin, are not sold and are not granted by this purchase: they are earned by clearing particular campaign levels, which the purchase makes reachable. The purchase is linked to your Google Play account and restores automatically on reinstall, or via the Restore purchases option in the in-game Settings. There is no subscription and no other purchase.

Earlier versions sold three smaller products (an ad-removal purchase and the two factions, then sold separately). Those are withdrawn; if you bought any of them, your account is recognised as owning the full game and nothing further is charged.

The in-game Grove Upgrades screen lets you spend Heartseeds you earn through gameplay on permanent unlocks. Heartseeds are an in-game currency only; they are not purchased with real money and have no monetary value. Everything you earn during the free preview is kept if you later buy the full game.

Google Play Games Services

Rootfast optionally integrates with Google Play Games Services for sign-in, achievements, leaderboards, and cloud saves (Saved Games). Rootfast does not create or maintain its own user accounts; signing in uses your existing Google account, which is created and managed by Google. Sign-in is optional — if you do not sign in to Google Play Games, your progress is stored only on your device and is not synced to the cloud, and Play Games profile data is not shared. However, other integrated Google services (Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics) may still process data as described elsewhere in this policy, subject to your diagnostics choice.

If you do sign in, Google Play Games Services collects the following for app functionality purposes:

  • User ID (Player ID) — a unique identifier linking your Google account to your in-game profile. If you have also turned on Share anonymous diagnostics, this identifier is additionally used as the user identifier in Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics (see those sections for the conditions and how to withdraw).
  • Saved game data (app activity) — your stored game progress, synced through Google Play Saved Games so it can be restored on reinstall or a new device.
  • Achievements and leaderboard scores — achievements you unlock and scores you submit are associated with your Play Games profile; leaderboard scores may be visible to other players depending on your Google Play Games privacy settings.

Rootfast uses Google Play Games Services to provide sign-in, achievements, leaderboards, and cloud saves. The one further use is the Player ID described above: for signed-in players who have turned on diagnostics, it also identifies your activity within Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics, so that gameplay and crash data can be grouped per player rather than per install.

Your Saved Games snapshot is never sent to Firebase — the stored file holding your progress goes only to Google Play Saved Games, and neither analytics nor crash reporting receives it. That is not the same as saying Firebase sees nothing about your progress: if you have turned on diagnostics, the events described under Firebase Analytics above separately record overlapping facts, such as which level you played, how it ended, the stars and score you earned and how far through the campaign you are. The difference is that those are individual game events, not your saved file, and they are not written back into it. Achievement unlocks and leaderboard scores are likewise held by Play Games; Firebase records only the app events listed above. Tinker Apps does not store any of this information on its own servers or use it for any other purpose. Google Play Games Services is operated by Google under Google's Privacy Policy, with additional information at support.google.com/googleplaygames.

Firebase Analytics

Rootfast uses Google Firebase Analytics to understand how players interact with the game. Firebase Analytics may collect:

  • App events — for example: starting and ending a run, waves cleared and where a run was lost, campaign level results and stars, tutorial progress, cards chosen or skipped during a draft, Grove upgrade unlocks, whether the purchase screen was shown and whether it was dismissed, in-app setting changes, and errors saving your game to the device
  • Purchase events, including the amount and currency of an in-app purchase you complete, so that revenue can be reported alongside gameplay. Tinker Apps never receives, sees, or stores your payment details — see Google Play Billing below.
  • Device information (model, OS version, language)
  • A randomly-generated app instance ID
  • Approximate location derived from IP address
  • Your Play Games Player ID, but only if you are signed in to Google Play Games and have consented — see below

Player segments. Alongside individual events, Rootfast records a small set of coarse labels describing what kind of player you are, so that the reports above can be broken down by group rather than read as one undifferentiated total. For Rootfast these are: how many runs you have played, your total stars, how many Grove upgrades and card combinations you have unlocked, how far through the campaign you have reached, which additional factions you own, whether you own the full game, and whether the developer cheat menu has been used.

Most of these are deliberately coarse ranges rather than exact values (for example "5–19 runs", not "eleven runs"). Two are recorded as exact counts — your total stars and the number of Grove upgrades you own — because the ranges were too wide to be useful for balancing the game's difficulty. All of them describe your activity in this game only, none of them is used to target advertising, and they are only recorded at all if you have turned on Share anonymous diagnostics.

Play Games Player ID in analytics and crash reports. If you are signed in to Google Play Games, Rootfast may pass your Play Games Player ID to Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics as a user identifier. This lets us tell that events or a crash report came from the same player across sessions and devices, so we can diagnose a problem you report to us and understand how progression works over time rather than only per install. Specifically:

  • The Player ID is a pseudonymous identifier issued by Google and scoped to this game. It is not your name, email address, Google account address, or Play Games display name, and none of those are ever sent to Firebase.
  • It is only used after you turn on Share anonymous diagnostics. Until you answer that question — and if you decline — no Player ID is attached to analytics events or crash reports.
  • If you turn it off (Settings > Share anonymous diagnostics), the identifier is cleared from both services immediately and stops being attached to anything new.
  • Where Google Play indicates that a user is a minor (see Age Signals below), no Player ID is attached, regardless of consent.
  • If you never sign in to Google Play Games, there is no Player ID and nothing is attached.

This data is processed by Google under Google's Privacy Policy. Tinker Apps uses analytics reports and aggregated insights generated from this data to understand gameplay trends, improve the app, and evaluate features, and does not use Firebase Analytics to directly identify individual users by name, email, or similar directly identifying information. Where a Play Games Player ID is attached as described above, activity can be linked to that pseudonymous identifier — and so, by Google, to the Google account behind it — but Tinker Apps sees only the identifier itself, and uses it to group a player's own activity together, not to build advertising profiles. In the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, Firebase Analytics does not store or use device identifiers until you consent through Google's consent message; if consent is not given, analytics identifiers remain disabled (Analytics may still record limited, non-identifying information needed for basic operation). You may also be able to limit certain data collection through your device or Google privacy settings.

Firebase Crashlytics

Rootfast uses Google Firebase Crashlytics to automatically detect and report app crashes and errors. When a crash or reported error occurs, Crashlytics may collect:

  • Stack traces and crash or error logs
  • Device state at the time of the crash (device model, OS version, available memory)
  • Installation and diagnostic identifiers used by Firebase Crashlytics to associate crash reports with a particular app installation
  • Context about where in the game the problem happened — the screen you were on, and the mode, level, and wave of any run in progress
  • Your Play Games Player ID, on the same consent-gated basis described under Firebase Analytics above (signed in and consented only; never for minors; cleared if you withdraw consent). Crash reports are still collected without it.

This data is used solely to diagnose and fix bugs and crashes. It is not used for advertising or profiling, and Tinker Apps does not receive your name, email address, or Play Games display name — where a Player ID is attached, it lets us match a crash to a problem you report to us directly. Crashlytics is operated by Google under Google's Privacy Policy.

Google Play In-App Review

Rootfast may occasionally invite you to rate the game using the Google Play In-App Review API, which shows Google Play's standard rating dialog inside the app. This flow is provided and operated by Google Play: Google processes the data needed to display the dialog and, if you choose to submit a rating or review, it is associated with your Google account under Google Play's own terms and privacy policy. The app itself never sees whether you rated it or what you wrote, and the prompt appears at most rarely (you can always dismiss it). The Rate this app option in Settings simply opens the app's Google Play store listing.

Age Signals & Age-Appropriate Experiences

To support applicable U.S. state app-store age-verification laws (such as the Texas App Store Accountability Act, SB 2420), Rootfast uses the Google Play Age Signals API. Where required by law in your region, Google Play may verify a user's age and provide the app with an age category (for example, an age range such as 0–12, 13–15, 16–17, or 18+) and parental-consent or supervision status.

Rootfast uses this information only to provide an age-appropriate experience — specifically, where Google Play indicates a player is a minor, the app does not ask the diagnostics question at all and withholds the Play Games Player ID from analytics and crash reports — and for legal compliance and safety, as permitted by these laws. The app carries no advertising, so this signal no longer affects any ad treatment; earlier versions used it to restrict ad personalisation and to offer minors an ad-free break in place of an interstitial, and both of those went with the ads. In-app purchases are not restricted by the app based on this signal; purchases by minors are gated by Google Play's own parental-approval controls (such as Family Link / "Ask to Buy"). Tinker Apps does not use this age information for any other purpose and does not store it on its own servers. Age verification and parental consent are handled by Google Play. This signal is provided only for users in regions where it is legally required; in all other cases the app applies its standard settings.

Data Retention

Tinker Apps does not retain gameplay-related personal data on its own infrastructure. Retention periods for data processed by Google services are determined by Google's service settings and policies and may change over time. In general:

  • Firebase Analytics — event-level data is retained for a limited period configured within Firebase; aggregated data may be retained for longer. See Firebase's privacy and security documentation.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — Firebase documents that Crashlytics crash traces and their associated identifiers are retained for 90 days before removal begins.
  • Google Play Games Services — saved game, achievement, leaderboard, and profile data is retained until you delete it via your Google account.

Local game data is retained on your device until you uninstall the app or clear app data in your device settings. Any support correspondence you send us is retained only as long as needed to handle your request.

Data Deletion

Tinker Apps does not store gameplay-related personal data on its own infrastructure. To manage or remove data:

  • Local game data — uninstall the app or clear app data in your device settings. The in-game Settings > Reset progress option erases your Heartseeds, unlocks, campaign progress and any run in progress, and — if you are signed in to Google Play Games — pushes that erased state to your cloud save, so it clears there too. ⚠️ It does not revoke your purchase, does not change your diagnostics choice or sound settings, and does not delete analytics or crash records already sent to Google; see the two entries below for those.
  • Cloud saved games, achievements & leaderboard data — delete via your Google Play Games profile or Google Account settings under Settings > Delete Play Games account and data.
  • Firebase Analytics & Crashlytics data — processed by Google. Some data may be associated with device- or app-level identifiers rather than your Google Account and may not be individually accessible or deletable by Tinker Apps. Where your Play Games Player ID has been attached (signed in, with diagnostics turned on), analytics data associated with that identifier can be located: email us from any address with your Player ID — visible in the Play Games app under your profile — and Tinker Apps will submit a user-deletion request for that identifier in Firebase Analytics. We ask for nothing else to verify a request, because the Player ID is the only thing that identifies the records and asking for more would collect data we do not otherwise hold. ⚠️ Turning the diagnostics toggle off stops your Player ID being attached to anything new; it does not by itself delete records already uploaded, which is what this request is for. For information about Google's privacy controls, see Google's Privacy Policy.

Some data collected by third-party services (such as Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics) may not be individually identifiable or deletable, but is used in aggregate and retained according to Google's data retention policies.

To request deletion of data associated with your use of Rootfast, or for help with any of the steps above, contact us at tinkerapps.support@gmail.com. We will respond to and assist with deletion requests where reasonably possible, or direct you to the appropriate Google privacy controls.

Your Choices

You have several controls over the data processed through the app:

  • Turn analytics and diagnostics off (or on) — open Settings > Share anonymous diagnostics in the app, at any time and in any region. Turning it off takes effect immediately and clears your Play Games Player ID from Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, so it stops being attached to new events and crash reports.
  • Avoid the Player ID entirely — do not sign in to Google Play Games. The game is fully playable signed out; you lose cloud saves, achievements, and leaderboards, and no Player ID exists to attach.

  • Manage data held by Google — at myaccount.google.com.
  • Remove local game data — uninstall the app, clear app data in your device settings, or use the in-game Settings > Reset progress option.
